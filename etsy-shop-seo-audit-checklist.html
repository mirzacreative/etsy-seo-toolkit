Etsy Shop SEO Audit Checklist

Updated 16 November 2026 · 9-minute read

Quick answer: An Etsy shop SEO audit checks every part of your shop that affects search: listing titles, all 13 tags, photos, descriptions, attributes and materials, pricing and shipping, shop title and settings, reviews, and your stats. Work through the checklist below listing by listing, score each item pass or fail, and fix the failures on your best sellers first. Most shops find the same problems: titles that describe the product instead of matching buyer searches, half-empty tag slots, one or two photos instead of ten, and a shop title that says nothing about what the shop sells. Fix those four and most shops see more search visits within a few weeks.

Why audit your shop instead of guessing

When Etsy sales slow down, most sellers guess. They lower a price, run a sale, or list a new product — and nothing changes, because the problem was never the price. The problem was usually that the listing stopped matching what buyers search for, or that a newer listing from a competitor now ranks above it. An audit replaces guessing with evidence. You open each listing, check it against a fixed list, and write down exactly what fails. After an hour you know which listings need new titles, which need photos, and which are fine and should be left alone. The audit also stops you from fixing things that are not broken: many sellers rewrite listings that were already ranking well, and lose the ranking they had. Audit first, fix second, and only fix what the checklist says is failing. Step 1: Audit your listing titles

Open your ten best sellers and read each title as a buyer would. The first 55 characters matter most, because that is what shows in search results — so the main keyword must come first, not last. A title that starts with "Handmade personalised ceramic mug, blue speckled glaze, gift for mum" is doing the job; a title that starts with "Beautiful unique gift for her, handmade with love" is not, because nobody searches for "beautiful unique gift". Check for repeated words: Etsy ignores duplicates, so a title that says "mug" three times wastes space that could hold another keyword. Check for keyword stuffing too — a title crammed with unrelated search terms looks spammy and puts buyers off. Each title should read like a clear description of the product, with the most-searched words first. Step 2: Audit all 13 tags

Every listing gets 13 tag slots, and every empty slot is search visibility you threw away. Open each listing and count: are all 13 filled? Are any tags duplicated, or repeated from the title word for word? Etsy reads tags and titles together, so a tag that repeats the title exactly adds little — use the tag slots for different words buyers might search for, including synonyms, occasions, and the person the gift is for. Check for single-word tags, which are usually too broad: "mug" is weak, "personalised ceramic mug" is strong. Check for tags longer than 20 characters, which Etsy cuts off. And check that every tag describes something actually in the listing — misleading tags bring the wrong buyers, who leave without buying, and that hurts the listing's ranking. Step 3: Audit your photos

Photos decide the click, and the click decides the sale, so audit them honestly. Open each listing on your phone, the way a buyer sees it. Does the first photo read clearly as a small square thumbnail? Is the product in focus, well lit, and filling the frame? Are all ten photo slots used? Check for the shots that answer buyer questions: a scale shot, a lifestyle shot, detail close-ups, a size comparison, and the back or second angle. If a listing has one photo, that is the first fix. If the first photo has text on it — a sale banner, a discount, a watermark — remove the text, because it clutters the thumbnail and looks unprofessional in search. Check that the photos match the product as it is sold now, not an older version with a different colour or design. Step 4: Audit descriptions, attributes and materials

The description is where the buyer decides, and where Etsy reads more keywords. Check that the first two sentences say what the product is, who it is for, and why it is worth the price — not a long story about your inspiration. Check that the description answers the questions buyers actually ask: size, materials, colours, personalisation options, dispatch time, and what is included in the parcel. Check the attributes: are the colour, size, and occasion fields filled in? Etsy uses attributes as search filters, so an empty attribute means the listing never appears when a buyer filters by it. Check the materials list: real materials, spelled correctly, not generic words. And check that the description does not contradict the photos — a different colour, a size the photos do not show, or a personalisation option the photos never mention. Every contradiction is a reason for a buyer to leave, and a reason for a bad review later.

Step 5: Audit pricing, shipping and shop settings

SEO is not only about words. Check your prices against similar listings: far above the market without a clear reason, and buyers scroll past; far below, and buyers suspect the quality. Check shipping: is the processing time realistic, are the costs shown clearly, and do you offer the delivery options buyers in your main markets expect? Check your shop title — it should say what you sell in plain words, because it appears in search results and on your shop page. Check your shop announcement and your About section: both should be current, and the About section should include the keywords that describe your products, because Etsy indexes it. Check your shop policies: clear returns, dispatch and personalisation policies reduce messages and increase buyer confidence. Check your profile photo and shop banner: a shop that looks finished is trusted more than a shop that looks abandoned, and trust affects whether a buyer clicks at all.

Step 6: Audit reviews, stats and dead listings

Open your shop stats for the last 30 days and look at three numbers per listing: views, favourites, and conversion rate. A listing with views but no favourites has a photo or price problem. A listing with favourites but no sales has a description or shipping problem. A listing with no views at all in 90 days has a search problem — new title, new tags, new first photo, in that order. Read your recent reviews for patterns: if three buyers mention slow dispatch, fix dispatch before you fix anything else. If buyers keep asking the same question, the answer belongs in the description. Finally, check for dead weight: listings that have not sold in a year and get no views. Either rewrite them completely or deactivate them — a shop full of listings that never sell looks worse in search than a smaller shop where every listing earns its place. Run this audit every three months, and a quick version once a month, so small problems never grow into a quiet shop.

Score your shop and fix in the right order

Score each listing as you go: one point for a passing title, one for full tags, one for ten photos, one for a complete description and attributes, and one for correct pricing and shipping. A listing that scores five is done — leave it alone. A listing that scores three or less needs work, and the order matters: fix titles first, because they decide whether the listing is found at all; then tags, because they decide which searches it appears in; then the first photo, because it decides the click; then the description, because it decides the sale. Fix your ten best sellers first, then work through the rest in batches of ten a week. Do not rewrite everything in one day — changing every listing at once makes it impossible to tell which fix actually helped. Fix, wait a week, check the stats, and then fix the next batch. That is how an audit turns into growth instead of a weekend of busywork.

Mistakes that make an audit useless

  • Fixing before auditing — rewriting listings at random, including ones that were already ranking well.
  • Only checking your favourite listings — the problems are usually in the listings you forgot about.
  • Leaving tag slots empty — every empty slot is search visibility thrown away.
  • Titles that describe, not match — buyers search for products, not poetry.
  • One photo per listing — buyers assume you are hiding something.
  • Ignoring the stats — views, favourites and conversion rate tell you which fix each listing needs.
  • Changing everything at once — you cannot tell which fix worked, so you cannot repeat it.
  • Never repeating the audit — search changes with the seasons; a shop audited once a year slowly drifts out of step.

Your 60-minute audit routine

Set a timer and work in order. Ten minutes: open your stats, list your ten best sellers and your ten worst listings by views. Twenty minutes: audit titles and tags on the ten best sellers, and write the new titles and tags for any that fail. Fifteen minutes: audit the first photo and the photo count on those ten listings, and note which need reshooting. Ten minutes: check the shop title, announcement, About section and policies. Five minutes: write down the fixes in priority order — titles first, then tags, then photos, then descriptions. Do the fixes over the next few days, not in the same sitting, and check the stats a week later to see which listings moved. Then repeat the audit on the next batch of ten. After three months, every listing in the shop will have been checked, fixed, and measured — and you will know, from your own numbers, what your buyers search for and what makes them buy. Frequently asked questions

How often should I audit my Etsy shop SEO?
Run a full audit every three months, and a quick version once a month. Etsy search changes with the seasons, your listings age, and new competitors appear. A quarterly audit catches the listings that have slipped, the tags that no longer match what buyers search for, and the shop settings you forgot to update.

What is the most common Etsy SEO problem an audit finds?
Titles that describe the product instead of matching what buyers type into search. Sellers write titles like "Beautiful handmade gift for her" while buyers search for "personalised ceramic mug for mum". The fix is simple: put the words buyers actually search for at the start of the title, and keep the poetic words for the description.

Should I audit every listing or just my best sellers?
Start with your ten best sellers, because fixing those has the biggest effect on your shop. Then work through the rest in batches of ten, one batch a week. Listings with no views in 90 days deserve a full rewrite — new title, new tags, new first photo — because something in the listing is not matching what buyers search for.

How long does an Etsy SEO audit take?
About an hour for a small shop with under 30 listings, and two to three hours for a larger shop. The audit itself is the fast part — reading the checklist against each listing. The fixes take longer, so score everything first, then fix the highest-impact problems: titles and tags on your best sellers, then photos, then the rest.
