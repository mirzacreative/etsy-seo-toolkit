Etsy SEO for New Shops: Get Your First Sales

Updated 17 November 2026 · 9-minute read

Quick answer: A new Etsy shop gets its first sales by being findable. Set a shop title that says what you sell, write an About section with your main keywords, and list at least ten products — each with a keyword-first title, all 13 tags filled, ten photos, and a clear description. Price fairly, not cheapest, and ask your first buyers for a review. Most new shops fail on visibility, not on product quality: Etsy cannot show a listing it cannot understand, and a listing with an empty tag slot or a vague title is a listing Etsy cannot understand.

Why new shops struggle — and what actually fixes it

Opening an Etsy shop is easy. Getting the first sale is the hard part, and the reason is simple: a new shop has no sales history, no reviews, and no ranking. Etsy search favours listings that have already sold, because a listing with sales is a listing Etsy knows buyers want. A new listing has to earn its place with the only signals it has — its title, its tags, its photos, its description and its shop settings. That is good news, because those are all things you control. The shops that get stuck are usually the ones that list two or three products with one photo each and wait. The shops that start selling are the ones that treat the first ten listings as a proper launch: every listing complete, every tag filled, every photo deliberate. This guide walks through that launch in order, from the shop settings to the tenth listing, and finishes with the pricing and review habits that turn a first sale into a second one. Step 1: Set up the shop title and About section

Before your first listing, finish the shop itself. The shop title should say what you sell in plain words — "Handmade Ceramic Mugs and Personalised Gifts" tells a buyer and Etsy exactly what the shop is, while "Mugs & Things by Sarah" tells them nothing. Etsy shows the shop title in search results and on the shop page, and it indexes the words in it, so your main product keywords belong there. The About section is where you tell your story, but it is also indexed, so include the words that describe your products and the people who buy them: the materials you use, the occasions your products suit, and what makes them handmade or personal. Add a clear shop banner and a profile photo — a shop that looks finished is trusted more than a shop that looks half-built, and trust affects whether a visitor clicks a listing at all. Fill in the shop policies too: returns, dispatch times, and how personalisation works. Buyers read policies before they buy from an unknown shop, and clear policies answer their doubts before they become messages. Finally, check the shop announcement: it should be current, not a launch message from three months ago, and it can carry a short note about dispatch times or a seasonal offer.

Step 2: Plan your first 10 listings

Aim for ten listings before you judge the shop. Ten listings give Etsy ten chances to show your shop in search, and they give a visitor a reason to stay and browse — a shop with one or two listings looks unfinished and gives buyers very little to buy, even if those listings are good. Plan the ten before you publish them. List your best sellers first, not your experiments: the products you can make well, photograph well, and deliver reliably. Vary them enough that they cover different searches — different colours, different occasions, different gift recipients — so each listing targets a different set of keywords instead of ten listings competing for the same search. Check that you can actually fulfil each one: the materials are in stock, the personalisation options are clear, and the dispatch time is realistic. A new shop that sells out of a listing in its first week loses momentum; a new shop that delivers its first ten orders on time earns its first reviews. Write the list on paper first — product, main keyword, price, and the photo you will lead with — and only then start building the listings one by one.

Step 3: Write listing titles buyers actually search for

The listing title is the single most important SEO field you control, and for a new shop it matters even more, because the title is what Etsy reads first. Put the words buyers type into search at the start of the title, not at the end: "Personalised ceramic mug for mum, handmade birthday gift" will be found; "Beautiful unique gift for her, handmade with love" will not, because nobody searches for "beautiful unique gift". The first 55 characters matter most, because that is what shows in search results — so the main keyword goes first, and the poetic words go later or into the description. Do not repeat the same word three times: Etsy ignores duplicates, and the space is better used for another keyword. Do not stuff the title with unrelated search terms either — a title crammed with words that have nothing to do with the product looks spammy, puts buyers off, and brings the wrong visitors who leave without buying. Each title should read like a clear description of the product, with the most-searched words first. Step 4: Fill all 13 tags — every slot, every listing

Every listing gets 13 tag slots, and every empty slot is search visibility you threw away. For a new shop, tags are the cheapest ranking tool you have, because they cost nothing and they directly decide which searches your listing appears in. Fill all 13 on every listing. Use the tag slots for different words from the title — synonyms, occasions, materials, and the person the gift is for — because Etsy reads tags and titles together, and a tag that repeats the title word for word adds little. Prefer multi-word phrases over single words: "personalised ceramic mug" is a stronger tag than "mug", because it matches a real buyer search instead of a million competing listings. Keep each tag under 20 characters, because Etsy cuts off longer ones. And make sure every tag describes something actually in the listing — misleading tags bring the wrong buyers, who leave without buying, and that hurts the listing's ranking. If you are stuck for tag ideas, search Etsy for your product and read the titles of the best sellers: the words they use are the words buyers use. Step 5: Photos that win the click

Photos decide the click, and the click decides the sale, so a new shop cannot afford weak photos. Use all ten photo slots on every listing. The first photo is the one that appears in search, so it has to read clearly as a small square thumbnail: the product in focus, well lit, and filling the frame. Then answer the buyer's questions in the remaining photos: a scale shot showing the size, a lifestyle shot of the product in use, detail close-ups, a size comparison, the back or second angle, and the packaging. Do not put text on the first photo — no sale banner, no discount, no watermark — because it clutters the thumbnail and looks unprofessional in search results. Check every photo on your phone, the way a buyer sees it, not only on your computer. And make sure the photos show the product as it is sold now, not an older version with a different colour or design — every contradiction between photo and parcel is a reason for a bad review. Step 6: Descriptions, attributes and materials

The description is where the buyer decides, and where Etsy reads more keywords. The first two sentences should say what the product is, who it is for, and why it is worth the price — not a long story about your inspiration. Then answer the questions buyers actually ask: size, materials, colours, personalisation options, dispatch time, and what is included in the parcel. Fill in the attributes: colour, size, occasion and any other fields Etsy offers for your category, because Etsy uses attributes as search filters — an empty attribute means the listing never appears when a buyer filters by it. List the real materials, spelled correctly, not generic words. And keep the description consistent with the photos: a different colour, a size the photos do not show, or a personalisation option the photos never mention will cost you the sale or earn you a bad review. Write the description for a buyer who has never heard of your shop, because for a new shop, that is every buyer.

Step 7: Pricing for a new shop

Pricing is where new sellers make their two biggest mistakes: pricing too low to win sales, and pricing too high without a reason. Too low and buyers suspect the quality — and you earn so little per order that ten sales still do not pay for the materials. Too high without a clear reason and buyers scroll past to a similar listing at a fair price. The fix is to price against the market, not against your nerves. Search Etsy for products like yours, note the price range of the listings that actually sell, and place yourself inside that range — fairly, not cheapest. Include your costs properly: materials, your time, Etsy fees, and packaging and postage. A small opening offer can help win the first reviews, which matter more than the discount itself: a small percentage off, or free shipping on a first order, is enough. Keep it modest and make sure the discounted price still leaves you a profit. A deep discount that loses money on every order is not a strategy, it is a leak. And do not change your prices every week — Etsy and buyers both prefer a stable price, and constant changes make it impossible to tell whether a price or a listing is the problem.

Step 8: Getting reviews — and what to do after the first sale

Reviews are the signal a new shop needs most, because they are the proof the next buyer looks for. After each order is delivered, send a short, friendly message thanking the buyer and asking them to leave a review if they are happy with the product. Do not offer a reward for a review, and do not ask for a five-star review specifically — ask for an honest one. Most buyers are happy to leave a review when asked politely and at the right time, which is a day or two after delivery, not the day the order is placed. Read every review for patterns: if buyers mention slow dispatch, fix dispatch; if they keep asking the same question, the answer belongs in the description. A single bad review in a new shop hurts, so reply to it calmly and specifically, and fix the problem behind it. Meanwhile, keep the shop active: add a new listing every week or two, renew listings that are about to expire, and check your shop stats for which listings get views and which get none. A listing with views but no sales has a photo or price problem; a listing with no views at all has a search problem — new title, new tags, new first photo, in that order. Once the first ten listings are live and the first reviews are in, run the full Etsy shop SEO audit checklist to find what still needs fixing, and repeat it every three months.

Mistakes that keep new shops at zero sales

  • Listing one or two products and waiting — a shop with almost nothing to buy gives buyers almost nothing to find.
  • Leaving tag slots empty — every empty slot is search visibility thrown away.
  • Titles that describe, not match — buyers search for products, not poetry.
  • One photo per listing — buyers assume you are hiding something.
  • Pricing far below the market — buyers suspect the quality, and you earn nothing per order.
  • An unfinished shop page — no banner, no About section, no policies, and buyers do not trust it.
  • Never asking for reviews — reviews only grow when someone asks for them.
  • Changing everything after one quiet week — new listings need time to be found; change one thing at a time and measure it.

Your first-month launch plan

Week one: finish the shop — title, About section, banner, photo, policies — and plan your ten listings on paper, with the main keyword and price for each. Week two: build the first five listings completely — keyword-first titles, all 13 tags, ten photos, full descriptions and attributes — using the free Etsy title and tag generator to write the titles and tags. Week three: build the remaining five listings the same way, and check every listing on your phone the way a buyer sees it. Week four: check your shop stats — which listings get views, which get favourites, which get nothing — and fix the weakest listing first: title, then tags, then first photo. Send a thank-you message to every buyer after delivery and ask for an honest review. Then repeat: one new listing a week, one stats check a week, and a full audit every three months. The first sale is the hardest one to win, because it has to be earned with a complete shop and complete listings. After that, every sale, every review and every new listing makes the next sale a little easier. Start with the shop title today, and publish your first complete listing tomorrow.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take for a new Etsy shop to get its first sale?
It varies a lot, but a shop with ten well-written listings, full tags and good photos usually sees its first sale within a few weeks, while a shop with one or two listings and empty tag slots can wait months. The first sale is mostly a visibility problem: once Etsy can find your listings in search, buyers can find them too.

How many listings does a new Etsy shop need?
Aim for at least ten listings before you judge the shop. Ten listings give Etsy ten chances to show your shop in search, and give a visitor a reason to stay and browse. A shop with one or two listings looks unfinished and gives buyers very little to buy, even if those listings are good.

Does a new Etsy shop rank lower in search?
A new shop starts with no sales history and no reviews, which are signals Etsy uses in ranking, so new listings often rank below established ones at first. You can offset that with complete, well-written listings: strong titles, all 13 tags, ten photos, and accurate attributes. Fresh listings also get a short period of extra visibility, so publish your best work first.

Should a new Etsy shop offer a sale or discount?
A small opening offer can help a new shop win its first reviews, which matter more than the discount itself. Keep it modest — a small percentage off, or free shipping on a first order — and price the product so the discount still leaves you a profit. A deep discount that loses money on every order is not a strategy, it is a leak.
