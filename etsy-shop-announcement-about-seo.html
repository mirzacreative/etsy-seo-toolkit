Etsy Shop Announcement & About: Write It for SEO

Updated 18 November 2026 · 9-minute read

Quick answer: Your Etsy shop announcement and About section are indexed by Etsy, so the words in them help your shop appear in search — but only if they are the words buyers actually search for. The announcement should be one or two sentences with your main product words and the most useful current information, such as dispatch times or a seasonal note. The About section should say who you are, what you make, the materials you use and who your products are for, using the same product words buyers type into search. Both fields have to work for two readers at once: Etsy, which reads the words, and the buyer, who reads the sentences. Keyword-stuffed text fails the buyer; a poetic story with no product words fails Etsy.

Why these two fields matter more than most sellers think

Most Etsy sellers spend hours on listing titles and tags, and then leave the shop announcement blank or write "Welcome to my shop!" in the About section. That is a missed opportunity, because both fields are indexed: Etsy reads the words in them when it decides which searches your shop can appear for. They are not as powerful as a listing title or a tag — a single listing with a strong title will always outrank a shop page — but they are free, they cost nothing to fill, and they are the first thing a visitor sees when they land on your shop. The announcement sits at the top of the shop page, above everything else. The About section is where a buyer who is deciding whether to trust an unknown shop goes to check who is behind it. Together they do two jobs: they add searchable product words to your shop, and they turn a stranger into a buyer. A shop with a clear announcement and a real About section looks finished and trustworthy; a shop with an empty announcement and a one-line About section looks abandoned, even if its listings are good. What the shop announcement is — and where buyers see it

The shop announcement is a short message that appears at the top of your shop page, above your listings. Every visitor sees it before they scroll, which makes it the most visible text on your shop — and also the easiest to waste. Etsy gives you limited space, so the announcement has to earn every word. Use it for information that is true right now: current dispatch times, a seasonal note ("Christmas orders close 15 December"), a short offer, or a note about a temporary delay. Do not use it for information that never changes, and never leave an old announcement in place — a launch message from three months ago, a Christmas note still showing in February, or a sale that ended weeks ago all tell the buyer the shop is not being looked after. Update the announcement at least once a month, and immediately whenever something changes: a holiday closure, a dispatch delay, or a new seasonal offer. Think of it as the sign on a shop door: it should tell people what is happening today, not what happened when the shop opened.

What belongs in the announcement — and what does not

A good announcement has three ingredients: your main product words, one useful piece of current information, and nothing else. Start with the product words, because those are the words Etsy indexes: "Handmade ceramic mugs and personalised gifts — dispatched within 2 working days." That one sentence tells Etsy what the shop sells, and tells the buyer when their order will leave. Other things that belong: a seasonal deadline, a short offer with a clear end date, or a note about a temporary change such as a holiday closure. Things that do not belong: a long story about your inspiration (that goes in the About section), a list of every product you sell (that is what your listings are for), a plea for reviews, or a wall of hashtags. Hashtags do nothing in a shop announcement, and they make the shop look unprofessional. Do not repeat your shop name — the buyer can already see it. And do not use the announcement to apologise for a delay that has already passed, or to explain a policy that belongs in your shop policies. Keep it to one or two sentences, and read it back as a buyer: does it tell you what the shop sells and what is happening right now? If it does both, it is done.

The About section: your story, written for search

The About section is where you tell your story — but it is also indexed, so the story has to include the words that describe your products and the people who buy them. Start with the facts a buyer actually wants: who you are, what you make, and how it is made. "I make handmade ceramic mugs in my studio in Yorkshire, thrown on the wheel and glazed by hand" tells the buyer everything they need to know in one sentence, and it contains the product words Etsy reads. Then add the details that build trust: the materials you use, how long you have been making, and what makes your work different from a factory product. Then the practical details buyers ask about: personalisation options, dispatch times, and what is included in the parcel. A short story about why you started is good — buyers on Etsy like buying from a real person — but keep it brief and real. A genuine two-sentence story beats a long, vague paragraph about "passion" and "craftsmanship" that could describe any shop. And use the words buyers actually search for: if you sell personalised gifts for mums, say "personalised gifts for mum" in plain words, not "bespoke keepsakes for the maternal figure in your life". The About section should sound like a person talking about their work, with the product words woven in naturally — never a list of keywords separated by commas.

Keywords that belong in these two fields

Choose your keywords the same way you choose listing keywords: from what buyers actually search for. Your main product words come first — the product itself, the material, and the person or occasion it is for. For a mug seller, that might be "handmade ceramic mug", "personalised mug", and "birthday gift for mum". For a jewellery seller, "handmade silver necklace", "personalised name necklace", and "gift for her". Put the most important product words in the announcement, because it is short and every word counts. Use the About section for the wider set: materials, occasions, and the people who buy from you, spread through normal sentences. Use each important phrase once or twice — not five times. Etsy reads the words, and repeating a phrase over and over adds nothing; it just makes the text unreadable. Do not include words for products you do not sell, even if they are popular searches — a buyer who arrives looking for something you do not sell leaves without buying, and that hurts the shop. And do not copy another shop's announcement or About section: your words should describe your products, your materials and your story, because that is what makes them true — and true words are the ones buyers trust. What buyers actually read — and what they skip

Buyers do not read your shop page the way you do. They scan it in seconds, looking for answers to four questions: what does this shop sell, is it trustworthy, when will my order arrive, and can I personalise it? The announcement answers the first and third questions in one glance — which is why it should lead with product words and dispatch times. The About section answers the second question, but only if the buyer actually opens it — and most buyers only open it when they are already interested in a listing and want to check who is behind it. That means the About section is not a sales pitch; it is a trust check. A buyer who opens it is looking for signs of a real person: a real name, a real place, real materials, real photos of the work in progress. Stock photos, vague claims and copied text fail that check. The things buyers skip: long introductions, lists of awards nobody has heard of, and paragraphs about your philosophy. The things buyers read: the first sentence, the materials, the dispatch time, and the personalisation options. Write for the scanner, not the reader: short paragraphs, plain words, and the important facts early. If the announcement and About section answer the four questions in a few seconds, they have done their job.

Mistakes that waste the announcement and About section

  • Leaving the announcement blank — the most visible text on your shop is empty, and Etsy has nothing to index there.
  • A stale announcement — a launch message from months ago, or a Christmas note still showing in February, tells buyers the shop is not looked after.
  • "Welcome to my shop!" — it says nothing about what you sell, and Etsy cannot index it into any useful search.
  • Keyword stuffing — a list of keywords separated by commas looks spammy, puts buyers off, and adds nothing beyond the first mention of each word.
  • A story with no product words — a poetic About section that never says what you actually make helps nobody find the shop.
  • Copying another shop's text — it describes someone else's products, and buyers can tell it is not yours.
  • Hiding the practical details — dispatch times, personalisation options and materials belong in these fields, not buried in a policy page.
  • Promising what you cannot deliver — "dispatched in 24 hours" when it takes a week earns bad reviews, not sales.

Fill-in examples you can adapt

Use these as starting points, not as text to copy word for word — replace every detail with your own products, materials and times. The structure is what matters: product words first, then the useful current information.

Announcement example 1 — a mug seller: "Handmade ceramic mugs and personalised gifts — dispatched within 2 working days. Christmas orders close 15 December."

Announcement example 2 — a jewellery seller: "Handmade silver necklaces, personalised with a name or date. Free UK delivery on orders over £30 — this week only."

Announcement example 3 — a print seller: "Printable wall art and nursery prints — instant download after purchase. New Christmas collection added this week."

About section example — opening paragraph: "I'm Sarah, and I make handmade ceramic mugs in my small studio in Yorkshire. Every mug is thrown on the wheel, glazed by hand, and made to order — personalised with a name, a date, or a short message for birthdays, weddings and new homes. I started making mugs five years ago, after a pottery course I took as a hobby, and I still make every order myself. Orders are dispatched within 2 working days, carefully packed, and every mug is checked before it leaves the studio."

Notice what the example does: it names the maker, the product, the material, the method, the personalisation options, the occasions, and the dispatch time — in normal sentences, with the product words appearing naturally. Your version should do the same for your products. Write it in your own voice, with your own details, and read it aloud before you save it: if it sounds like a person talking about their work, it is right.

Keeping both fields fresh

These two fields are not write-once-and-forget. The announcement should change at least once a month, and immediately when anything changes — a holiday, a dispatch delay, a new seasonal collection, or an offer ending. The About section needs less frequent attention, but review it every three months: are the materials still right, are the dispatch times still accurate, do the occasions you list still match what you sell? Update it when your products change, when you add a new range, or when buyers keep asking the same question — because a question asked three times belongs in the About section, where the next buyer can find it before asking. Add a photo or two to the About section as well: your workspace, your hands at work, a finished product. Photos make the story believable, and a believable story is what turns a browser into a buyer. The announcement and About section are small fields, but they are the first words a buyer sees and some of the easiest words Etsy can index. Write them properly once, keep them current, and they will work for the shop every day.

Frequently asked questions

Does the Etsy shop announcement help with SEO?
Yes, modestly. Etsy indexes the words in your shop announcement, so it can help your shop appear for the product words it contains. It is not as powerful as listing titles or tags, but it is a free, indexed field that many shops leave blank or fill with a stale launch message — so using it properly is an easy advantage.

What should I write in my Etsy About section?
Write who you are, what you make, the materials you use, and who your products are for — using the same words buyers search for. Add your story briefly, then the practical details: how products are made, dispatch times, and personalisation options. Keep it honest and specific: a real story about your craft beats generic claims.

How long should an Etsy shop announcement be?
Short — one or two sentences is usually enough. The announcement shows at the top of your shop, so buyers see it before they scroll. Put the most useful information first: dispatch times, a seasonal note, or a current offer. Anything longer belongs in the About section or your shop policies.

Should I put keywords in my Etsy shop title and About section?
Yes, but naturally. Use the main product words once or twice in normal sentences — for example, "handmade ceramic mugs, personalised for birthdays" — rather than listing keywords separated by commas. Etsy reads the words, and buyers read the sentences, so the text has to work for both.
